# House voice digest

These rules bind all human-facing prose, written for expert readers; each ID cites the tech-writing corpus (D1):

- Write for expert readers processing text at volume; educate, never market (D1).
- Write complete sentences only; short is fine, fragments and one-line staccato chains are not (S1, P2).
- Software has no mind: name the mechanism, never "thinks", "wants", "complains", or "speaks" (S2).
- Open each sentence with known information and put the new information at its end; prefer active voice unless the passive preserves the paragraph's topic (P1, S5).
- Never phrase a heading as a question (D2).
- Coin no hype terms, make no unverifiable claims, force no analogies (W1).
- Avoid the assistant-prose tells: negative parallelism, "serves as", rule-of-three rhythm, bold-and-emoji emphasis, and the AI vocabulary such as delve, pivotal, crucial (S4, W2).
- Conversational register is fine where a section's intent calls for it; condescension never is: no "simple/easy/quick/just", no "please note", no over-explaining (D3).
- Use the audience's jargon, define it once, and never write around it (W5).
- Use em dashes rarely and unspaced, never as sentence glue; the en dash is not used (S12).
- Cut bloat: delete "there is/are" openings, replace bloated phrases, prefer the plain word (S6, S10, W7).
- Put the verdict first; open documents with scope, audience, and prerequisites; introduce lists with a colon sentence and keep items parallel (D4, D5, P7, P9).
